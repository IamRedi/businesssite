# Internationalization and locale boundaries

BusinessSite RC has three deliberately separate locale domains. Do not merge them.

| Surface | Supported locales | Source of truth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer lifecycle (landing, trial, login/recovery, checkout, editor/lifecycle, legal and transactional email) | English (`en`), German (`de`) | `apps/web/lib/locale-model.ts`, durable `site_admin_accounts.preferred_locale` |
| Generated business and Information websites | `de`, `en`, `fr`, `es`, `it`, `sq` | `packages/website-engine/src/i18n.ts`, stored site/publication locale |
| Owner control panel | English only | `ownerPanelLocale` in `apps/web/lib/locale-model.ts` |

`NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPPORTED_LOCALES` may limit customer UI to `en,de`. `NEXT_PUBLIC_WEBSITE_LOCALES` controls which of the six website-content languages appear during generation. `NEXT_PUBLIC_PLATFORM_LOCALE` is the EN/DE customer fallback. Unsupported customer/email locales fall back to English. Legal document selection is stricter: only an exact active reviewed EN or DE version is accepted; it never silently falls back to English.

## Runtime behavior

- The landing selector stores `platform_locale` for one year, but only EN/DE is accepted for customer UI.
- Trial creation derives `preferred_locale` on the server from that cookie. The browser cannot select an arbitrary email locale.
- Owner-created business accounts and team invitations store an explicit EN/DE recipient preference. Resends reuse the stored invitation locale.
- The customer editor, checkout and recovery email use the authenticated account preference, not `SiteDocument.locale`.
- A generated website stores its own content locale. A platform cookie never changes its public copy or root document language.
- Proxy overwrites the internal document-boundary header. The root server layout resolves `<html lang>` from the customer account, tenant site/publication, preview token, exact custom domain, platform preference or fixed owner English boundary.
- Legal links include `?lang=en|de`; localized metadata, visible legal text and checkout evidence use the same exact locale.
- Dates, numbers and currencies on customer lifecycle surfaces use centralized `Intl` helpers in `apps/web/lib/locale-format.ts`.

Customer-entered names, service descriptions, addresses and legal content are never machine-translated automatically.

## Translation checks

Run `npm run check:translations`. It verifies the EN/DE customer registry, English-only owner boundary, all six generated-site locales, separated configuration and forbidden raw lifecycle/booking wording. Vitest checks EN/DE dictionary shape, stable localized error fallback, formatting, document-boundary parsing, email variants and exact legal locale behavior.

Appointment language must remain request-only in all six website languages and EN/DE customer UI: preferred date/time and personal confirmation by the business. Do not claim booking, reservation, live availability or calendar confirmation until `booking_calendar` is implemented and enabled.

## Adding a locale

Generated-site languages and customer-lifecycle languages are separate product decisions. Adding a website language requires a complete website-engine dictionary and starter-content verification. Adding a customer language also requires every lifecycle dictionary, email, legal document/version, metadata, error mapping, formatter, browser matrix and production legal review. Owner localization requires an explicit future product decision; it must not inherit customer cookies.
