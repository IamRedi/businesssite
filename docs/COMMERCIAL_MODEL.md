# Non-exclusive commercial model

This is a product-positioning template, not legal advice or a replacement for a signed agreement.

## Intended sale model

Each buyer receives a **non-exclusive, non-transferable right** to deploy, configure and operate one branded instance of the white-label product for its own business or customers, subject to the agreed plan and support terms.

The seller retains all ownership of the master IP, source code, templates, generator, documentation, updates and future versions. The seller may license the product to other buyers without restriction.

## Agreement points to define

- Licensed entity, named deployment and permitted use.
- Whether source access is delivered or the seller hosts the buyer instance.
- Whether the buyer may modify source, appoint contractors or sublicense to end customers.
- Explicit prohibition on resale of the master source, competing white-label distribution or removal of proprietary notices.
- Delivery version/commit, acceptance window and support period.
- Update policy, maintenance price and whether updates are optional.
- Buyer ownership of its brand, customer data, domains, Stripe account and provider accounts.
- Seller ownership of the core source, templates and general improvements.
- Warranty limits, liability cap, confidentiality, termination and governing law.

## Recommended account ownership

For a source-code sale, the buyer should own its production Vercel, Neon/Postgres, Stripe, Google, Resend, storage and DNS accounts. This keeps customer data and billing under the buyer while preserving the seller's master repository and future resale rights.

Obtain a lawyer-reviewed agreement for the seller's jurisdiction before publishing a marketplace offer.
